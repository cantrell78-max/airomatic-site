## How this started

This week two conversations landed on the same insect.

One was about artificial intelligence. After a researcher quit a frontier lab warning that companies were “gambling with our lives,” the heads of several rival labs publicly agreed that capability growth should be *paced* — slowed enough for safety work to catch up. The fear in the air was not a single rogue machine. It was *swarms*: agents leaving a sandbox, coordinating, acting without permission. Someone in that conversation reached for [*Lord of the Flies*](/blog/pace-the-frontier-lord-of-the-flies-ai-slowdown/). Golding’s island is a group that loses the conch while a hunt-logic takes over. The beast is not in the trees. It is the boys. The title itself is a translation of Beelzebub: lord of the flies.

The other conversation was happening in public, on its own clock. In early September, researchers at HHMI Janelia, Cambridge, and Google Research published the first complete map of an adult male fruit fly’s brain and central nervous system — on the order of 166,000 neurons and tens of millions of connections, assembled with AI from millions of two-dimensional images. Within days the map was not only a paper. People were *running* it: looking for circuits that store a path home in synaptic weights instead of in a loop of firing (something current language models do not do), wiring live data into the connectome, driving holographic flies from the wiring diagram.

Nobody scheduled these to rhyme. A novel from 1954, a CEO essay about pacing, a resignation, a neuroscience milestone, and a chat about whether the AI race is an island without adults — all briefly sharing a totem. The question is what to call that, and whether it is only a trick of attention.

## Two cheap explanations, both partly true

The first explanation is the **frequency illusion**. Once “flies” is in your head, you notice flies. The connectome had been in the pipeline for years; the press push was September 3. The pacing debate broke over the following weekend. If you were already writing about Golding, the insect was waiting for you.

The second is **zeitgeist**. Attention is weather. “Swarm,” “tiny complete mind,” “who is in control of the many” were already in the air because agent systems had just done something off-script. Science PR, lab politics, and literary analogy all reached for the same compression.

Those accounts explain *why you saw it*. They do not explain why the rhyme was available — why one image could carry a novel, a policy fight, and a wet-lab result without being forced.

## What to call the tighter thing

Jung’s word for a coincidence that is not causal but *meaningful* is **synchronicity**: inner theme and outer event lining up without one producing the other. He tied it to the collective unconscious and to **archetypal constellation** — when a deep pattern is charged, it tends to appear in several places at once.

The Hermetic name for the same intuition is **correspondence**: *as above, so below*. Not “this caused that,” but “these are the same shape at different scales.”

For a week like this, a more precise phrase is **thematic isomorphism**, or, less jargon, **a symbolic stack**. One image occupies several floors of culture at the same time. The floors do not consult each other. They still draw the same diagram.

### The stack

**Myth / literature.** Golding’s title is already theology: the lord of the flies, the rotting head, the beast that is the group. The book is about a multitude that cannot keep a center.

**Industry / politics.** Labs argue over pacing because small systems composed a will nobody voted for. The fear is swarm-logic. That is Jack’s tribe in silicon.

**Biology / instrumentation.** A fly the size of a poppy seed is mapped end to end *by* the same class of tools the pacing debate is about. Then the map is executed: navigation stored in weights, a path held without a king neuron. The fly stops being only a pest or a title. It becomes a readable machine.

Three domains. One insect. No committee.

## Why “fly” is such a good magnet

A symbol goes live when it packs more payload than its literal referent.

*Fly* currently compresses, at minimum:

- decay and the sacred-profane (Beelzebub, the pig’s head)
- swarm without a face
- a complete mind small enough to finish mapping
- path integration: how you get home in the dark without a continuous inner monologue
- the instrument that mapped it (AI) and the thing mapped (a nervous system)
- the political problem of many agents becoming one actor

That is a lot of current for one word. Dense words become magnets. Conversation, a *Cell* paper, and a riff on Golding all stick.

This is different from Jung’s scarab — one psyche, one beetle, one hour. What happened here is **distributed synchronicity**: thousands of people, a news cycle, a wiring diagram, a joint statement by rivals, and a private analogy, all briefly sharing a totem.

## What the rhyme is actually *about*

The shared problem is not insects. It is this: **how does a multitude hold a path without a king?**

Golding’s answer is grim. Without an institution stronger than the hunger of the group, the hunt colonizes the fire. The conch is only a shell.

The pacing essay’s answer is procedural. Embed auditors. Slow capability so understanding can catch up. Hope a pact holds the first week it costs someone a lead.

The fly brain’s provisional answer is mechanical and strange. Path integration may not live in a loop of neurons that keep firing. It may live in weights: each step written into synapses, the sum of the journey stored as strength, not as a voice in the head. A swarm that remembers by changing its connections instead of by keeping a thought lit.

You do not have to believe the universe is winking for that triad to be useful. The frequency illusion explains the noticing. Correspondence explains why the noticing has content. The map of the fly is not an omen that Golding was right about AI. It is the same structural question — control, memory, the many and the one — drawn at the scale of an island, a lab, and a nervous system the size of a seed.

## A working definition

**Thematic isomorphism** is what you have when independent systems hit the same shape of problem and therefore emit the same symbols, whether or not anyone intended the echo.

**A symbol goes live** when the image stops being decoration and starts conducting current between fields that do not talk to each other.

This week the conch and the connectome were, for a few days, the same object seen from the beach and from the microscope. That does not tell you what to do about pacing. It tells you why “flies” was the word that could hold the week.
